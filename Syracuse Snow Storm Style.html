Dress for the wind, not the number.

Syracuse winters are long, windy, and snowy. This guide gives you six simple steps for building an outfit that keeps you warm and dry on the walk to class, and still looks like you.

What this site teaches

You will learn how to check the forecast, layer your clothes, choose a coat, protect your hands and feet, and add pieces that feel like you. Every step has a photo and plain instructions you can follow in about ten minutes.

Who it’s for

This guide is written for Syracuse University students, especially first-years and students from warmer places who are dressing for a Syracuse winter for the first time. It also works for anyone who wants practical cold-weather outfit ideas.

What you’ll need

  • A winter coat, or a plan to buy one
  • A few warm layers you already own
  • Waterproof boots or shoes with good grip
  • About ten minutes

How to dress for a Syracuse winter

Follow these six steps to build an outfit that works for a walk to class. Jump to any step using the buttons below.

1

Check the forecast before you dress

Look at the temperature, the wind, and whether snow or rain is expected. Wind makes it feel colder than the number shows, so dress for the “feels like” temperature.

Tip: Check again right before you leave, since lake-effect snow can change quickly.

Add your photo: a screenshot of your weather app showing temperature and “feels like.”
2

Start with a warm base layer

Put on a thin, snug layer against your skin, such as a long-sleeve top, thermal shirt, or fleece-lined tights. It traps warmth and keeps you comfortable all day.

Tip: Avoid cotton if you expect to get wet, because it stays damp.

Add your photo: a flat lay of your base layer pieces.
3

Add an insulating middle layer

Layer a hoodie, sweater, or fleece over your base layer. This layer holds in your body heat, and you can take it off if your classroom is warm.

Tip: Choose a middle layer that is easy to take off and carry in your bag.

Add your photo: you pulling on a hoodie or sweater.
4

Choose a windproof, water-resistant outer layer

Finish with a coat that blocks wind and sheds snow, such as a puffer, parka, or insulated jacket. Zip it fully and pull the hood up if it’s windy.

Tip: A longer coat covers more of your legs when the wind picks up.

Add your photo: you zipping your coat outside, with snow behind you if possible.
5

Protect your feet, hands, and head

Wear waterproof boots with good grip and warm socks. Add a hat, gloves, and a scarf, because uncovered skin loses heat quickly and wet feet get cold fast.

Tip: Keep an extra pair of dry socks in your bag.

Add your photo: boots, gloves, hat, and scarf laid out together.
6

Add your personal style

Pick one or two pieces that feel like you, such as a colorful scarf, a beret, a statement belt, or a favorite bag. Warm clothing can still be coordinated and confident.
